Drive dynamics

The physics behind it

Newton's 2nd law along the road: m·a = Ftraction − Fdrag − Froll − m·g·sinθ − Fbrake

Traction is limited by motor torque at low speed, by power at high speed (F = P / v) and by tire grip (F ≤ μ·N).

Aero drag Fd = ½ ρ Cd A v² (ρ ≈ 1.225 kg/m³). Power to beat it grows with v³.

Rolling resistance Fr = Crr·m·g·cosθ (Crr ≈ 0.011 assumed for all-terrain tires).

Weight transfer ΔN = m·a·h / L — accelerating squats the rear, braking dives the nose; the low battery-floor CG keeps h small.

Kinetic energy KE = ½ m v² — what the brakes (or regen) must absorb. Stopping distance d ≈ v² / (2·abrake).

Steering uses a kinematic bicycle model: yaw rate ψ̇ = v·tanδ / L, lateral accel ay = v·ψ̇ (capped by grip), which rolls the body.

Why drag matters

Fd = ½ ρ Cd A v² and Pd = Fd·v ∝ v³. Doubling speed → 4× the force and 8× the power.

The Cybertruck's Cd is commonly cited around ~0.33–0.35 (approx.); frontal area here is estimated at ~3.35 m² and changes with ride height. Typical full-size pickups are often quoted around 0.4–0.5.

Lowering the air suspension at speed shrinks frontal area and under-body flow — that's why the truck auto-lowers on the highway.

Battery electrochemistry

Drive with W to discharge (ions flow graphite → NMC); lift off or brake for regen, or plug in, to watch them reverse. Charging runs as a 40× time-lapse.

Pack facts (approximate)
  • Cells: Tesla 4680 cylindrical cells (46 mm × 80 mm), ~1,336 in the pack, standing upright in a structural floor pack.
  • Energy: ~123 kWh usable (reported). Architecture: 800-V class. This sim assumes 216 cells in series (OCV ≈ 670–920 V).
  • Chemistry: high-nickel NMC-type cathode (LiMO₂, M = Ni/Mn/Co) and a graphite anode; liquid electrolyte (LiPF₆ salt in carbonate solvents).
  • Heat: Joule heating Q̇ = I²R with an assumed effective pack resistance of 0.08 Ω; liquid cooling ramps up above ~30 °C.
  • Terminal voltage sags under load: V = OCV − I·R; current from P = V·I.

Materials science

Why it doesn't rust

The exoskeleton is Tesla's cold-rolled "Ultra-Hard 30X" stainless steel. Its exact recipe is proprietary; austenitic stainless like 304 is roughly Fe (balance) · ~18% Cr · ~8% Ni with small Mn, Si, C.

With ≳10.5% chromium, Cr at the surface reacts with oxygen: 4 Cr + 3 O₂ → 2 Cr₂O₃. This passive film is only ~1–3 nm thick, dense and adherent, so it blocks further oxidation — and if scratched it re-forms in air (self-healing).

Plain carbon steel instead forms porous, flaky rust: 4 Fe + 3 O₂ + 2n H₂O → 2 Fe₂O₃·nH₂O, which lets corrosion keep going — hence paint.

Cold rolling multiplies dislocations (⊥) in the crystal; they tangle and block each other, raising strength and hardness (work hardening). The sheet is so hard it's impractical to stamp into curves — one reason for the flat, folded, faceted design.

Laminated "Armor Glass"

Tesla describes Armor Glass as a multi-layer laminate; the exact layup isn't public, so this cross-section is illustrative.

Glass plies are soda-lime glass (≈ 72% SiO₂, 14% Na₂O, 9% CaO + MgO/Al₂O₃) bonded by polymer interlayers such as PVB (polyvinyl butyral).

On impact the outer ply cracks, but the tough interlayer stretches, absorbs energy and keeps the shards bonded, so the projectile is spread over a larger area instead of punching through.
